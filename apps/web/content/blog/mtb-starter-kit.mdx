You do not need a garage full of carbon to start riding. You need a bike that fits, a helmet you will actually wear, and a short list of parts that keep you moving when something goes wrong on the trail.

This is that list. Every section links to **current** Dropper sale inventory — not a static “buy this SKU” that goes stale next week. Prices and stock change; confirm on the retailer before you pay.

## Who this is for

- **First bike.** You are stepping off a hybrid or a friend’s hand-me-down and want a sane kit, not a magazine “dream build.”
- **Returning rider.** You still have a frame in the rafters. Most of this still applies — especially helmet, tires, and a dropper if you never had one.

We are not picking a “best eMTB 2026.” If you want a price-banded browse, use the live hubs: [mountain bikes under $3,000](/deals/hub/mountain-bikes-under-3000) and [eMTBs under $5,000](/deals/hub/emtbs-under-5000).

## 1. The bike (or a used one that isn’t a project)

A complete [mountain bike](/deals/c/bikes/mountain) is the expensive line item. New on sale is fine. Used is fine if you can inspect the frame, headset, and pivots — we do not list used bikes, so that search stays local.

What matters more than the badge on the downtube:

- **Fit.** Stand-over and reach you can live with. Size charts lie; sit on it.
- **Brakes and tires you trust.** Those are cheaper to upgrade later than a cracked frame.
- **A dropper already on it** saves a Saturday and a few hundred dollars.

If the budget is tight, buy the used frame from a rider you trust and spend the sale money on the kit below.

## 2. Helmet — the one thing you do not cheap out on

A [helmet](/deals/c/gear/helmets) is not optional. Replace it after a real hit, and do not inherit one with unknown crash history.

Look for a current MIPS or equivalent rotational system, a fit that stays put when you shake your head, and coverage that matches how you ride (open-face for most trail; more coverage if you are pointed downhill). Visors and pads live in a different aisle — skip [helmet parts](/deals/c/gear/helmet-parts) until you own the lid.

## 3. Shoes that stay on the pedals

Trail [shoes](/deals/c/gear/shoes) with a stiff-enough sole beat running shoes the first time you mash a climb. Match the shoe to the pedal:

- **Flats** — sticky rubber, recessed tread. Forgiving when you are still finding the sweet spot.
- **Clipless** — two-bolt SPD-style. More efficient once you are used to clipping out.

Buy the pair you will actually walk in at the trailhead. Fashion colorways go on sale first; that is a feature.

## 4. Pedals that match those shoes

[Pedals](/deals/c/components/drivetrain/pedals) are the other half of the interface. Do not mix a road-style three-bolt shoe with a trail pedal and hope.

Flats: a wide platform and pins you can live with after a shin check. Clipless: a pedal that matches your cleat, with a release tension you can start easy.

## 5. Dropper post — the quality-of-life upgrade

If the complete bike did not come with a [dropper](/deals/c/components/cockpit/seatposts), this is the first cockpit part worth hunting on sale. Dropping the saddle for a descent is the difference between surviving a roll-down and walking it.

Check **travel** against your frame’s insertion depth and **diameter** (30.9 / 31.6 are common). Internal routing is cleaner; external still works on older frames. The embed below is seatposts — look for dropper travel in the title, or [search dropper](/deals?q=dropper).

## 6. Tires you can lean on

Stock [tires](/deals/c/components/wheels-tires/tires) are often the first thing a shop cheapens. A pair with the compound and casing for your dirt is cheaper than a washout.

Match **width** and **wheel size** (27.5 / 29) to the rim. If you are already tubeless, grab [sealant and valves](/deals/c/components/wheels-tires/tubeless) in the same cart.

## 7. Pack or hydration — and a multi-tool

You need a way to carry water, a tube or plug kit, and a [multi-tool](/deals/c/accessories/tools). A small [hip pack or frame bag](/deals/c/accessories/bags) is enough for most trail days; a [hydration pack](/deals/c/accessories/hydration) if you are out longer.

The tool should cover hex keys, T25, and a chain breaker. Add a [pump](/deals/c/accessories/pumps) if you do not already have one at home — floor pump for the garage, mini-pump or CO2 for the pack.

## What you can skip for now

Gloves, knee pads, and a second jersey are nice. They are not why you stay home. [Protection](/deals/c/gear/protection) and [lights](/deals/c/accessories/lights) can wait until you have a regular night or a bike-park day on the calendar.

Do not buy a “starter groupset” to rebuild a bike you have not ridden. Ride first. The sale feed will still be here.
