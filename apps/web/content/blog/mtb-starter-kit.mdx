You do not need a carbon quiver. You need a bike that fits, a helmet you will actually wear, and enough kit that a trailside problem does not end the day.

The rest is shops selling you a lifestyle.

This is the short list. The rails below are live sale inventory, cheapest first, capped at beginner money — not last year’s enduro closeout at seven grand. Stock moves. Confirm the price on the retailer before you pay.

## Who this is for

First bike. You are coming off a hybrid or a friend’s hand-me-down and want a sane kit, not a magazine dream build.

Returning rider. There is still a frame in the rafters. Most of this still applies — especially the lid, the tires, and a dropper if you never had one.

We are not picking a “best eMTB.” If you want a price band, use the hubs: [mountain bikes under $1,000](/deals/hub/mountain-bikes-under-1000), [under $3,000](/deals/hub/mountain-bikes-under-3000), [eMTBs under $5,000](/deals/hub/emtbs-under-5000).

## 1. The bike (or a used one that isn’t a project)

A complete [mountain bike](/deals/c/bikes/mountain) is the expensive line. New on sale is fine. Used is fine if you can inspect the frame, headset, and pivots. We do not list used — that search stays local.

Forget the badge on the downtube. Fit first. Stand-over and reach you can live with. Size charts lie; sit on it.

Brakes and tires you trust matter more than a cracked-frame special with a fancy sticker. A dropper already on the bike saves a Saturday and a few hundred bucks.

Tight budget? Buy the used frame from a rider you trust. Spend the sale money on the kit below.

## 2. Helmet — the one thing you do not cheap out on

A [helmet](/deals/c/gear/helmets) is not optional. Replace it after a real hit. Do not inherit one with unknown crash history.

You want a current MIPS (or equivalent) rotational system, a fit that stays put when you shake your head, and coverage that matches how you ride — open-face for most trail, more shell if you are pointed downhill. You do not need a $400 carbon lid to start.

Visors and pads live in a different aisle. Skip [helmet parts](/deals/c/gear/helmet-parts) until you own the lid.

## 3. Shoes that stay on the pedals

Trail [shoes](/deals/c/gear/shoes) with a stiff-enough sole beat running shoes the first time you mash a climb. Match the shoe to the pedal.

**Flats** — sticky rubber, recessed tread. Forgiving while you find the sweet spot.

**Clipless** — two-bolt SPD-style. More efficient once you can clip out without thinking about it.

Buy the pair you will actually walk in at the trailhead. Fashion colorways go on sale first. That is a feature.

## 4. Pedals that match those shoes

[Pedals](/deals/c/components/drivetrain/pedals) are the other half of the interface. Do not mix a road-style three-bolt shoe with a trail pedal and hope.

Flats: a wide platform and pins you can live with after a shin check. Clipless: a pedal that matches your cleat, release tension you can start easy.

## 5. Dropper post — the quality-of-life upgrade

If the complete bike did not come with a [dropper](/deals/c/components/cockpit/seatposts), this is the first cockpit part worth hunting on sale. Dropping the saddle for a descent is the difference between surviving a roll-down and walking it.

Check **travel** against your frame’s insertion depth and **diameter** (30.9 / 31.6 are common). Internal routing is cleaner. External still works on older frames.

The rail is seatposts — look for dropper travel in the title, or [search dropper](/deals?q=dropper&sort=price_asc&max_price=200).

## 6. Tires you can lean on

Stock [tires](/deals/c/components/wheels-tires/tires) are often the first thing a shop cheapens. A pair with the compound and casing for your dirt is cheaper than a washout.

Match **width** and **wheel size** (27.5 / 29) to the rim. Already tubeless? Grab [sealant and valves](/deals/c/components/wheels-tires/tubeless) in the same cart.

## 7. Pack or hydration — and a multi-tool

You need a way to carry water, a tube or plug kit, and a [multi-tool](/deals/c/accessories/tools). A small [hip pack or frame bag](/deals/c/accessories/bags) covers most trail days. A [hydration pack](/deals/c/accessories/hydration) if you are out longer.

The tool should cover hex keys, T25, and a chain breaker. Add a [pump](/deals/c/accessories/pumps) if you do not already have one at home — floor pump for the garage, mini-pump or CO2 for the pack.

## What you can skip for now

Gloves, knee pads, and a second jersey are nice. They are not why you stay home. [Protection](/deals/c/gear/protection) and [lights](/deals/c/accessories/lights) can wait until you have a regular night or a bike-park day on the calendar.

Do not buy a “starter groupset” to rebuild a bike you have not ridden. Ride first. The sale feed will still be here.
